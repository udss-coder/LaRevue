The Kemoway 2026 e-bike lineup, compared

Five electric bikes, five different lives — the city hub-motor cruiser, the do-it-all trekking bike, the fold-and-go commuter, the family cargo hauler, and the all-rounder that started it all. Here's what each one actually costs, what it's good at, and where it falls short.

R By the La Reveu Editors
All products featured here are independently selected by our editors and writers. If you buy something through links on our site, La Reveu may earn an affiliate commission.

There is a version of city life that runs on electric assist now — the commute that no longer arrives sweaty, the grocery run that no longer needs the car, the weekend ride that goes twice as far without twice the effort. Kemoway has spent over a decade building toward exactly that shift. What began in 2011 as a small mobility project has grown into a catalogue of pedelecs sold across Europe, each one built around the same 720Wh battery platform but tuned for a different kind of rider.

We lined up five models from the current Kemoway range — the K5R, K6S, Z3, P7, and K5 — and compared them the way we compare anything else in this journal: on price, on what the spec sheet actually means for daily use, and on who each bike genuinely suits. This isn't a ranking. A folding commuter and a cargo bike are built for entirely different mornings, and the "best" Kemoway is simply the one that matches yours.

"The right e-bike isn't the one with the biggest numbers on the spec sheet — it's the one you'll actually reach for on a grey Tuesday morning." — La Reveu Editors

No. 01 in the edit
The City Entry Point

Kemoway K5R — the lightest way into the range

The K5R is Kemoway's answer to "I just want an e-bike that works." It skips the rear shock and the heavier mid-drive hardware found on the K6S and K5, and uses a simpler hub motor instead — which is exactly why it's the most affordable bike in the lineup and the lightest to carry up a flight of stairs.

A front suspension fork takes the edge off potholes and curbs, while the 720Wh battery — the same capacity used across nearly every Kemoway model — gives it real everyday range despite the more modest hub motor. It's built as a city bike first: fenders, a rack-ready frame, and 27.5-inch wheels that roll comfortably over cobblestones and bike lanes alike.

No. 02 in the edit
The All-Terrain Trekking Bike

Kemoway K6S — the one built for longer, rougher rides

Sold elsewhere as a full trekking / mountain-style e-bike, the K6S steps up to a mid-drive motor with a substantial 110 N·m of torque — twice what the K5R offers — making it the pick for hillier commutes, gravel paths, or weekend rides that leave the pavement altogether.

The same 720Wh battery pack carries over, but here it's paired with a more capable drivetrain built to handle sustained climbs without straining. At 28.5 kg it's heavier than the K5R, which is the trade-off for the added torque and a sturdier trekking frame — expect this one to live outside or in a garage rather than get carried up to a third-floor apartment daily.

The Fold-and-Go Commuter Currently sold out

Kemoway Z3 — built for the train, the boot, and the last mile

The Z3 is Kemoway's folding e-bike, designed for the commute that involves more than just pedaling — a train leg, a lift up to an apartment, a camper van's storage locker. It's the model the brand's own blog points to for combining cycling with public transit, and for camper-van trips where boot space is at a premium.

Despite the compact folded footprint, it keeps the same 720Wh battery and a 55 N·m motor tuned to the EU-standard 250W of continuous power and 25 km/h assisted speed — enough for confident city riding without the bulk of a full-size frame. It's currently sold out on Kemoway's own site, which tracks with how often folding e-bikes in this price bracket move.

The Cargo & Family Hauler Currently sold out

Kemoway P7 — for the school run and the grocery haul

The P7 is the outlier in the range: a genuine cargo e-bike, built with smaller 20-inch wheels for stability under load and a lengthened frame to carry kids, groceries, or gear that wouldn't fit on any of Kemoway's other models.

It pairs the same 110 N·m mid-drive motor found on the K6S and K5 with the largest 720Wh battery in the range, which matters more here than anywhere else — hauling extra weight draws down a battery faster than commuting solo. At 39 kg it's by far the heaviest bike in the lineup, which is simply the cost of the reinforced frame and cargo capacity; this isn't a bike you're carrying up stairs, it's one you're parking outside.

No. 05 in the edit
The Original All-Rounder Currently sold out

Kemoway K5 — the bike that anchors the range

The K5 is the model most independent reviewers have actually gotten hands-on with, and it's easy to see why it's held its place in Kemoway's lineup: a robust aluminum frame, a strong Ananda mid-motor rated at 110 N·m, and the kind of comfort touches — an adjustable stem, ergonomic grips, and a tool-free quick-release saddle — that make it feel more considered than its price suggests.

Independent testing found the K5 comfortable on both paved roads and light unpaved trails, helped by a suspension fork with meaningful travel and componentry from established brands like Suntour and Shimano. Kemoway advertises this city model with a maximum estimated range of up to 170 km on its lowest assist setting — a best-case figure, as with any manufacturer range claim, but a sign of how much the 720Wh battery is built to stretch under gentle use.

Which Kemoway actually fits your ride

Line the five up and a pattern emerges quickly: torque and weight move together. The K5R stays light and affordable by using a simpler hub motor, which is exactly why it suits flat, short city commutes better than steep ones. The K6S, P7, and K5 all step up to a 110 N·m mid-drive motor — the same core strength, applied to three different jobs: distance and terrain on the K6S, cargo capacity on the P7, and all-around comfort and range on the K5. The Z3 solves a different problem entirely, trading some ride comfort for a frame that folds small enough to make a train platform or a camper van's storage locker part of your commute.

None of that makes one bike objectively "better" than another — it makes the choice mostly about your own week. Whichever model you land on, every Kemoway in this edit shares the same 720Wh battery platform and the same EU-standard 250W, 25 km/h pedal-assist limits, so the meaningful differences come down to torque, weight, and what the frame is actually built to do.

Pricing, availability, and stock status change frequently — always confirm current figures on Kemoway's official site before purchasing. Range estimates are based on manufacturer figures under ideal, low-assist conditions and will vary with rider weight, terrain, and weather.
